# VPAF source notes

This file is the only source for framework wording. `vpaf.v1.json` and `questions.v1.json` are written from what is recorded here.

**Status: DRAFT proposed by Claude on 2026-11-16. Not yet confirmed by Merstra.**

Merstra supplied no criteria, so the behaviours and proofs below were drafted from general venture-building practice, not from any published framework and not from the legacy app's descriptors. They only become Merstra's criteria once Merstra has edited and approved each row. Edit this file freely; the JSON is regenerated from the approved wording.

Stages are numbers only. Bands: Explore (1-3), Validate (4-6), Scale (7-9).

For each stage: **Behaviour** is what the venture has observably done. **Strong proof** counts on its own. **Weak proof** counts, but scores one stage lower.

## Customer (CUS)

Who the customer is, whether the problem is real for them, and whether they commit and pay.

| Stage | Band | Behaviour | Strong proof | Weak proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explore | The team has written down who it believes has the problem, without yet speaking to any of them. | Written concept note | — |
| 2 | Explore | The team has held first conversations with people in the target group and recorded what they said. | Interview log or synthesis | Founder's own notes |
| 3 | Explore | The team has named one specific customer group and the problem it faces, based on recorded conversations. | Written customer profile, Interview log or synthesis | — |
| 4 | Validate | Repeated interviews with the chosen customer group show the same problem, and the team can state why they would switch. | Interview log or synthesis | Written customer profile |
| 5 | Validate | Target customers have used a version of the product, and their feedback is recorded and acted on. | User feedback record, Usage data export | Interview log or synthesis |
| 6 | Validate | At least one customer has signed a commitment to pilot or buy, with agreed measures of success. | Signed letter of intent, Signed pilot agreement | Email confirmation from customer |
| 7 | Scale | Customers have paid for the product, and the team can describe how each sale was won. | Invoice, Payment record, Signed customer contract | Sales pipeline report |
| 8 | Scale | Several customers have bought through the same sales steps, and each new customer is onboarded the same way. | Signed customer contract, Invoice | Sales pipeline report |
| 9 | Scale | New customers arrive at a predictable rate, existing customers stay or buy more, and some act as references. | Retention or repeat-purchase data, Customer reference or testimonial | Sales pipeline report |

## Product (PRD)

What has been built, how it has been tested, and where it has been shown to work.

| Stage | Band | Behaviour | Strong proof | Weak proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explore | The product exists only as a described idea, with nothing built or specified in detail. | Written concept note | — |
| 2 | Explore | The team has specified how the product will work and what a first build must show. | Design or specification document | Written concept note |
| 3 | Explore | A first build exists and runs, but it fails often and has only been tried by the team. | Prototype demonstration (video or link) | Internal test report |
| 4 | Validate | The build performs its main functions in the team's own tests, with results recorded. | Internal test report | Prototype demonstration (video or link) |
| 5 | Validate | The build has been revised after testing, and recorded results show it now fails less often. | Internal test report | Release or change log |
| 6 | Validate | The product has worked in conditions close to real use, outside the team's own workplace. | Field trial report, Third-party test report | Prototype demonstration (video or link) |
| 7 | Scale | Real users have operated the product in their own setting to do their actual work. | Deployment record, Usage data export | User feedback record |
| 8 | Scale | The product runs steadily for several users in different settings, with faults tracked and fixed. | Usage data export, Support and fault log | Release or change log |
| 9 | Scale | The product serves many users at once, with staffed support, maintenance routines and measured reliability. | Reliability or uptime report, Support and fault log | Usage data export |

## Business Model (BIZ)

Who pays, how much, what it costs to deliver, and how it reaches the market.

| Stage | Band | Behaviour | Strong proof | Weak proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explore | The team cannot yet say who would pay, how much, or why. | Written concept note | — |
| 2 | Explore | The team has written down who pays, what they pay for, and how money would be collected. | Business model draft | Written concept note |
| 3 | Explore | The team has listed its costs and a proposed price, but has not checked them against real figures. | Pricing document, Cost breakdown | Business model draft |
| 4 | Validate | The proposed price has been put to potential customers and adjusted based on their responses. | Interview log or synthesis | Pricing document |
| 5 | Validate | The team calculates cost and income per sale from real figures and has chosen its routes to market. | Unit economics model | Cost breakdown |
| 6 | Validate | Customers have paid the intended price in a pilot or test sale, and delivery steps are written down. | Invoice, Payment record | Pricing document |
| 7 | Scale | Sales come repeatedly through at least one channel, at a price that covers the cost of delivery. | Sales by channel data, Management accounts | Unit economics model |
| 8 | Scale | Margins hold steady as volume grows, and the team has a written plan for expanding delivery. | Management accounts | Unit economics model |
| 9 | Scale | The business grows through established channels with stable margins, and reports the same growth measures every period. | Audited accounts, Management accounts | Sales by channel data |

## Moat (MOT)

What stops others copying it: ownership, protection, know-how, and any other durable advantage.

| Stage | Band | Behaviour | Strong proof | Weak proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explore | The team has not considered what would stop others copying the product or who owns the work. | Written concept note | — |
| 2 | Explore | The team has started checking what similar solutions exist and what others have already protected. | Review of competing solutions and existing rights | Written concept note |
| 3 | Explore | The team has written down what is distinctive about its product and settled in writing who owns it. | Ownership or assignment agreement | Review of competing solutions and existing rights |
| 4 | Validate | The team has chosen how it will stay hard to copy, such as patents, secrecy, data, brand or exclusive agreements. | Written protection plan | Review of competing solutions and existing rights |
| 5 | Validate | The team is preparing its chosen protection, with drafts, records or contracts in progress. | Legal adviser's opinion or draft | Written protection plan |
| 6 | Validate | A first protection is in place, such as a filed application, and conflicts with others' rights have been checked. | Filing receipt, Licence or exclusivity agreement | Legal adviser's opinion or draft |
| 7 | Scale | Protection covers the main markets the business sells in, and each element is tied to a source of revenue. | Filing receipt, Grant or registration certificate | Written protection plan |
| 8 | Scale | Granted rights or exclusive agreements are in force, and partners have opened talks to license or rely on them. | Grant or registration certificate, Licence or exclusivity agreement | Review of competing solutions and existing rights |
| 9 | Scale | The business monitors and enforces its position, and that position measurably earns or protects revenue. | Licence or exclusivity agreement, Management accounts | Legal adviser's opinion or draft |

## Team (TEA)

Who does the work, how roles and ownership are settled, and how the team runs.

| Stage | Band | Behaviour | Strong proof | Weak proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explore | One person is doing the work alone, and key skills the venture needs are absent. | Team roster with roles and time commitment | — |
| 2 | Explore | The founder is in talks with possible co-founders or early members, with no roles agreed. | Meeting record | Team roster with roles and time commitment |
| 3 | Explore | A core group is working on the venture, though responsibilities overlap and time commitments differ. | Team roster with roles and time commitment | Meeting record |
| 4 | Validate | Each core member has a named responsibility, and the team meets on a fixed schedule to review progress. | Meeting record | Team roster with roles and time commitment |
| 5 | Validate | Ownership shares are agreed in writing, outside advisers contribute regularly, and planned hires are listed. | Founders' agreement, Cap table | Adviser agreement, Hiring plan |
| 6 | Validate | The team delivers against its plans most periods, and decisions follow written rules on who decides what. | Board minutes | Meeting record |
| 7 | Scale | Every key function has a capable owner, and the team reliably meets the dates it sets. | Organisation chart, Employment contract | Meeting record |
| 8 | Scale | New members are hired and onboarded through a set process, and individual performance is reviewed regularly. | Performance review record, Employment contract | Hiring plan |
| 9 | Scale | Leaders below the founders run their areas independently, and governance works without the founders in every decision. | Board minutes | Organisation chart |

## Capital (CAP)

Runway, funding plan, funder progress, and financial control.

| Stage | Band | Behaviour | Strong proof | Weak proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explore | The team does not know how much money it has, how much it needs, or how long it will last. | Written concept note | — |
| 2 | Explore | The team has estimated how much money it needs and drawn up a rough budget. | Budget | Written concept note |
| 3 | Explore | The team has a first pitch and a list of milestones, without a clear plan for who to approach. | Pitch deck | Budget |
| 4 | Validate | The team has a pitch backed by supporting figures and a list of funders it plans to approach. | Pitch deck, Cash flow forecast | Funder contact log |
| 5 | Validate | Conversations with funders have begun, and each milestone is linked to the amount of money it requires. | Correspondence with funders | Funder contact log, Cash flow forecast |
| 6 | Validate | The team has a complete data room and a stated use of funds, and could respond to an offer. | Data room index, Cash flow forecast | Pitch deck |
| 7 | Scale | Funders have made written commitments, and the team tracks how many months of cash remain. | Term sheet or commitment letter, Bank statement extract | Funder contact log |
| 8 | Scale | Money is in the bank from investment or steady sales, and spending is approved and reported under set controls. | Bank statement extract, Signed funding agreement, Management accounts | Cash flow forecast |
| 9 | Scale | The business can choose between several sources of money, and its forecasts are reviewed against actual results. | Audited accounts, Term sheet or commitment letter | Cash flow forecast |
